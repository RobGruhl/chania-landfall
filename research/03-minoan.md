# 03 — Minoan Chania (Kydonia): facts and story hooks

Research for the Chania walking tour. Researched 2026-10-02 under time pressure (~12 min).
Confidence tags: **[high]** = several solid sources agree or it is a primary text; **[medium]** = one good
source, or widely repeated but not checked against the primary publication; **[low]** = plausible, from memory or
a weak source; verify before printing. Things scholars argue about are marked **CONTESTED**.

Chronology shorthand used by archaeologists (dates are approximate and themselves debated):
Early Minoan (EM) c. 3100–2100 BC · Middle Minoan / Protopalatial c. 2000–1700 · Neopalatial (MM III–LM IB)
c. 1700–1450 · Late Minoan II–IIIA (Mycenaean-run Knossos) c. 1450–1300 · LM IIIB c. 1300–1190 · LM IIIC to c. 1070.

## 1. Minoan Kydonia under Kastelli hill

- **Kastelli hill**, the low rise behind the Venetian harbour (Kanevaro St runs along it), sits on top of
  Minoan Kydonia. People lived there almost continuously for about 5,000 years. The one gap is roughly
  1150–735 BC. [high] https://www.sia.gr/en/articles.php?tid=366
- **Excavation history:** Yannis Tzedakis began rescue digs at **Agia Aikaterini Square on Kanevaro St in 1964**.
  From **1970** the dig became the systematic **Greek-Swedish excavation**, directed by Tzedakis and Carl-Gustaf
  Styrenius. The main campaigns ran 1970–1987, with further seasons in 1989–90 and later. The
  **Danish Institute** joined in **2010**, so it is now Greek-Swedish-Danish. [high]
  https://www.ancientkydonia.gr/en/project/plateia-agias-aikaterinis/ ·
  https://ecsi.se/actaath-4-47/
- **Why it mattered:** these digs proved there was a major Minoan centre in *western* Crete. Before them, many
  scholars thought Minoan power was concentrated in the centre and east of the island. [medium]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastelli_Hill
- **Scale of the Agia Aikaterini dig:** about 600 m², with about 2 m of layers stacked on top of each other.
  **Building 1 (Oikia 1)** covers 225 m², had two storeys and 14 ground-floor rooms. Streets and small public
  squares were found around it. [high] https://www.ancientkydonia.gr/en/project/plateia-agias-aikaterinis/
- **Domestic detail (for Jamie):** a **clay hearth with a loom beside it** was found in a kitchen, and a jar
  held **about 3 kg of burnt legumes (vetch, lentils/peas)**, carbonised in the fire. One room (D) held decorated
  vessels, stone tools, clay sealings, seals, amulets and jewellery. House I alone produced 300+ complete
  vessels. [high] https://www.ancientkydonia.gr/en/project/plateia-agias-aikaterinis/ ·
  https://www.sia.gr/en/articles.php?tid=366
- **What a visitor sees today:** an open, fenced excavation in Agia Aikaterini Square (Kanevaro St), under a
  protective roof built **2005–2008**. It is viewed from the street. Smaller exposed sections lie elsewhere on
  Kastelli (e.g. Katre St). [medium for the roof dates, low for the Katre St detail]
  https://www.sia.gr/en/articles.php?tid=366
- **Destruction layers:**
  - **LM IB fire, c. 1450 BC.** Sources give c. 1480–1425. This is the same Crete-wide wave of destruction that
    ends the Neopalatial "palace" world. Survivors later partly repaired some rooms (A, I, Q of Building 1).
    [high] https://www.ancientkydonia.gr/en/project/plateia-agias-aikaterinis/
  - **LM IIIA destruction, c. 1250 BC** per SIA. Other sources describe LM IIIA rebuilding followed by fire. Then
    **LM IIIB revival**: Kydonia becomes a large trading and maritime centre, possibly the leading town of
    post-Knossos Crete, and is destroyed or abandoned **c. 1200 BC**. [medium; the exact sequence differs
    between sources] https://www.sia.gr/en/articles.php?tid=366 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kydonia

### Writing at Kydonia
- **Linear A (the undeciphered Minoan script):** Kydonia produced roughly **97–99 Linear A tablets**. That is
  the **second-largest Linear A archive after Hagia Triada**. Most are economic records of agricultural
  produce and livestock. The archive was found **1973**, burnt (and so baked hard) in the LM IB fire. Two
  tablets came from the upper storey of Building 1. [medium on the exact count]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kydonia · https://www.sia.gr/en/articles.php?tid=366 ·
  https://www.ancientkydonia.gr/en/project/plateia-agias-aikaterinis/
- **Linear B (Mycenaean Greek):** **five Linear B tablets were found at Agia Aikaterini in 1989–1990**. They are
  dated **LM IIIB1 (13th c. BC)**. Apart from Knossos, Chania is one of very few Cretan sites with Linear B
  (Knossos, Chania, Malia). [high for the find, medium for the "only since 1900" framing]
  https://www.sia.gr/en/articles.php?tid=366 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kydonia
- **The Zeus-and-Dionysos tablet, KH Gq 5:** a Chania Linear B tablet recording **honey** offerings. It reads
  roughly **di-wi-ja-de** ("to the sanctuary of Zeus"), **di-we ME+RI** ("to Zeus, honey", 1 amphora),
  **di-wo-nu-so ME+RI** ("to Dionysos, honey", 2 amphorae). Zeus and Dionysos appear together, about **800
  years before** classical Greek drama, and Dionysos gets *more* honey than Zeus. [medium for the reading, high
  that both gods appear] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mycenaean_deities ·
  https://talesoftimesforgotten.com/2023/03/30/the-shocking-true-origin-of-dionysos/ ·
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336990120_Zeus_and_Dionysus_in_the_Light_of_Linear_B_records
  - Caution: the idea that this shows Dionysos was *already Zeus's son* is an inference. **CONTESTED/speculative.**
  - di-wo-nu-so is also attested at **Pylos**, so Chania is not the only Bronze Age Dionysos. [high]
- **ku-do-ni-ja:** the name **Kydonia** appears in Linear B as *ku-do-ni-ja*. **Correction to the brief:** the
  clear attestations are on **Knossos** tablets, which record Kydonia as a place Knossos administered or traded
  with (sheep and wool among other entries). I could not confirm that the name appears on a tablet *found at
  Chania*. Say "Knossos's scribes wrote the town's name", not "a Chania tablet names itself". [high for Knossos,
  low for Chania] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kydonia
- **Exported "made in Kydonia" jars:** large **inscribed stirrup jars** (oil/wine transport jars painted with
  Linear B) found at **Thebes** and other mainland sites have been traced by clay analysis to **west Crete /
  the Chania area**. [medium] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thebes_tablets

### The Master Impression
- A **clay sealing from Kastelli**, dated to the **second half of the 15th c. BC** (c. 1450–1400). It was
  stamped by a **gold signet ring that has never been found**. It shows a **towering male figure with long hair,
  a Minoan kilt and a staff or spear, standing on top of a multi-storey building complex**. The complex has
  windows and **horns of consecration**, sits on a **rocky shore by the sea**, and is walled with gates. It was
  published by **Erik Hallager, *The Master Impression* (Göteborg, 1985)**, and is now the excavation's emblem
  and a star of the Chania museum. [high]
  https://www.ancientkydonia.gr/en/project/plateia-agias-aikaterinis/ ·
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-hellenic-studies/article/abs/hallager-e-the-master-impression-a-clay-sealing-from-the-greekswedish-excavations-at-kastelli-khania-studies-in-mediterraneam-archaeology-69-goteborg-astrom-1985-pp-75-30-plates-price-not-stated/E62F2B42B9FB9FD5FEF74A45AFDDD610
  - Interpretation **CONTESTED**: a ruler, a god (a "Master of the city"), or a ruler shown as divine. It is
    sometimes read as a "portrait" of Kydonia itself, which is possible but not provable.
- Also from the LM IB destruction: a **clay bar bearing twelve seal impressions (ten different)**. This is
  bureaucracy in clay. [high] https://www.sia.gr/en/articles.php?tid=366

### Kydonia in classical memory
- **Homer:** the *Odyssey* mentions **Kydonians "living around the streams of the Iardanos"** (3.292) and lists
  them among Crete's peoples (19.176). [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kydonia
- **"Three cities":** **Strabo (Geography 10.4.7)** calls **Knossos, Gortyn and Kydonia** the three most notable
  cities of Crete. [medium; checked from memory of the text, not fetched]
  https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Strab.+10.4.7
- **Quince:** the Greeks called the quince *kydonion mēlon*, the "Kydonian apple". The English "quince" and the
  botanical genus *Cydonia* both come from it. A link to the town is the traditional etymology. Some linguists
  suspect a pre-Greek word that was assimilated to the town name, so that is **mildly CONTESTED**. [high for the
  ancient association] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kydonia

## 2. Myth

- **King Kydon**, the city's founder. Versions differ: son of **Hermes or Apollo and Akakallis, a daughter of
  Minos**; or son of Tegeates (an Arcadian); or Minos founded the city himself. Local pride: a grandson of Minos
  founded Chania. [high that the versions exist] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kydonia
- **Minos, Labyrinth, Minotaur:** Minos's wife Pasiphae gave birth to the bull-headed Minotaur. Daedalus built
  the Labyrinth to hold it. Athens sent youths as tribute until Theseus killed it with Ariadne's thread. The
  story is set at **Knossos**, not Chania. [high] https://www.theoi.com/Ther/Minotauros.html
  - *Labyrinth* is often derived from *labrys* (double axe), "house of the double axe". This is **CONTESTED**;
    a Linear B word *da-pu₂-ri-to-jo* (Knossos tablet) may be the same word. [medium]
- **Talos, the bronze giant:** in **Apollonius of Rhodes, *Argonautica* 4.1638–1693**, Talos is the last of the
  bronze race. He was given to **Europa by Zeus** (some versions say made by **Hephaestus for Minos**) and
  **ran around Crete three times a day**, hurling rocks at approaching ships. He was invulnerable except for a
  **single vein at his ankle covered by thin skin**. **Medea** bewitched him with the evil eye and phantoms, and
  he **grazed his ankle on a rock; the ichor ran out "like molten lead"** and he crashed down. In
  **Apollodorus (1.9.26)** the vein is closed by a **bronze nail**, which Medea (or the archer Poias) pulled out.
  [high] https://www.theoi.com/Gigante/GiganteTalos.html · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talos
  - **The Colossus link:** Apollonius was "of Rhodes", so the poet who gave us Crete's bronze guardian is tied to
    the island of the **Colossus of Rhodes**, a bronze giant guarding a harbour (c. 280 BC, felled by an
    earthquake c. 226 BC). Apollonius was writing in the 3rd c. BC, the Colossus's own century. That parallel
    is a fair tour line. Saying Apollonius *modelled* Talos on the Colossus would be **speculation**; tag it [low].
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_of_Rhodes
  - **Coins:** **Phaistos** struck coins showing a **winged Talos throwing a stone** (c. 3rd c. BC). [medium]
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talos
- **Daedalus and Icarus:** Minos imprisoned the master craftsman; Daedalus made wax-and-feather wings. Icarus flew
  too near the sun and fell into the sea (the Icarian Sea near Ikaria). Daedalus reached Sicily, where Minos
  pursued him and was killed (scalded in a bath). [high] https://www.theoi.com/Heros/Daidalos.html
- **Britomartis / Diktynna, the west-Cretan "lady of the nets":** Strabo derives *Diktynna* from *diktyon*,
  "net". Minos chased her for nine months. She leapt from a cliff into the sea, was **caught in fishermen's
  nets**, and became a goddess. Her great sanctuary, the **Diktynnaion**, is at the **tip of the Rodopou
  peninsula** (Menies bay), visible across the gulf west of Chania. The oldest finds there are **late 7th/6th c.
  BC** (so it is not demonstrably Minoan). The temple was rebuilt under **Hadrian** (2nd c. AD) with a paved road.
  [high for the myth and site; the Minoan roots of the goddess are **CONTESTED/plausible**]
  https://www.theoi.com/Georgikos/Britomartis.html ·
  https://followinghadrian.com/2017/06/11/the-hadrianic-temple-of-diktynna-in-crete/ ·
  https://www.worldhistory.org/Britomartis/
- **Zeus born on Crete:** Rhea hid the infant from Kronos. The Kouretes clashed their shields to drown his
  crying, and the goat Amaltheia nursed him. The birth cave is the **Diktaean Cave (Psychro, Lasithi)** or the
  **Idaean Cave** (Mt Ida), depending on the author. Both are in central and east Crete. [high]
  https://www.theoi.com/Olympios/Zeus.html
- **Europa and the bull:** Zeus as a white bull carried the Phoenician princess Europa to Crete (Gortyn, under
  a plane tree that by tradition never sheds its leaves). Their sons were **Minos**, Rhadamanthys and Sarpedon.
  Europa's bull is on the **Greek 2-euro coin**. [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europa_(consort_of_Zeus)

## 3. The Minoan world (context)

- **Thalassocracy:** **Thucydides 1.4**: Minos was the first known to build a navy, ruled most of the Greek sea,
  colonised the Cyclades, and **cleared the sea of pirates** "so that his revenues might come in". [high for
  what Thucydides says] http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Thuc.+1.4
  - Whether a Minoan *naval empire* existed is **CONTESTED**. Modern scholars prefer cultural and commercial
    influence (the "Minoanisation" of the Aegean) to political rule.
- **Bull-leaping:** shown on the **Knossos "Toreador" fresco** and on seals and figurines. Figures somersault
  over a charging bull. White-skinned (conventionally female) and red-skinned (male) leapers both appear. Whether
  white skin = women here is **CONTESTED**. [high for the images]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull-leaping
- **Double axe (labrys):** a ubiquitous sacred symbol, found in votive bronze and gold, as a mason's mark, and in
  frescoes. Plutarch (*Greek Questions* 45) says *labrys* is a Lydian word. [high]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrys
- **Peak sanctuaries and sacred caves:** open-air shrines on mountain tops (e.g. Juktas near Knossos) received
  clay votive figurines of people, animals and limbs. Caves (Idaean, Diktaean, Kamares) received offerings
  across centuries. [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_religion
- **"Snake goddess" figurines:** faience figures from the **Knossos Temple Repositories (Evans, 1903)**, c.
  1600 BC, now in the Heraklion Museum. They were heavily restored. The **Boston ivory "snake goddess" is very
  likely a modern forgery**. Whether they show a goddess, priestess or votary is **CONTESTED**. [high]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_Goddess_figurines
- **Theran (Santorini) eruption:** one of the largest eruptions of the Holocene. Its date is **CONTESTED**:
  radiocarbon (including the buried olive branch) points to **c. 1600 BC**, the older archaeological chronology
  to **c. 1530–1500 BC**. It did **not** directly end Minoan civilisation, which carried on for a few
  generations. [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_eruption
- **Mycenaean takeover c. 1450 BC:** after the LM IB destructions, **Knossos is run by Greek-speakers writing
  Linear B**. Whether this came by invasion, a dynastic shift, or an opportunistic takeover after internal
  collapse is **CONTESTED**. [high for Linear B at Knossos after c. 1450]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization
- **Linear B as bookkeeping:** the Knossos archive is overwhelmingly about **sheep (on the order of 100,000
  recorded), wool and textile quotas for named women workers, olive oil, figs, spices, chariots, and offerings to
  gods**. The tablets were sun-dried clay that survived only because the palace burned. [high]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B
- **"Minoan matriarchy"** is **CONTESTED / largely rejected as stated**. Women are prominent in ritual art,
  but no evidence shows women ruled. It goes back to Evans and later to goddess-movement writers.
  **"Minoan peace"** (unfortified, peaceful Crete) is **CONTESTED**: weapons, possible fortifications and
  violent destructions exist. The Master Impression itself shows a *walled* town. [high that these are debated]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization

## 4. Archaeological Museum of Chania (Chalepa)

- **Address:** **15 Skra St, Chalepa**, about 25 min walk east of the old harbour, or a short taxi ride. It
  **opened in the new building on 16 April 2022**. The old museum (since 1962) was in the Venetian church/monastery
  of **San Francesco on Chalidon St**, in the old town. Don't send people there for the collection. The new
  building is by **Theophanis Bobotis & partners**: about 6,000 m², triangular, "emerging from the hillside" and
  projecting toward the sea. It shows 4,100+ objects. [high]
  https://greekreporter.com/2022/04/16/archaeological-museum-of-chania-reopens-in-new-premises/ ·
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_Chania
- **Minoan things to hunt for** (check the floor plan on arrival; I could not confirm which room each is in):
  - **The Master Impression** (Kastelli, 1450–1400 BC). It is tiny: a clay lump a few cm across. Look closely. [high]
  - **Linear A tablets from Kydonia** and **Linear B tablets** (including the Zeus/Dionysos honey tablet, if
    displayed). [medium that both are on display]
  - **Painted clay larnakes (coffins)** from the **LM III cemetery of Armeni**. They date **1400–1200 BC**, and
    one is decorated with a **hunting scene (dogs, stag/goats, a hunter)**. Note that Armeni is near **Rethymno**,
    not Chania. [high for an Armeni larnax in the museum; medium for the hunt-scene description]
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_Chania
  - **Kydonia finds:** LM IIIB pottery (the "Kydonia workshop", exported widely), inscribed stirrup jars, seals and
    sealings, the clay bar of seal impressions. [medium]
  - **Early Minoan bird-shaped vessels** and **Cycladic-type cups (3200–2500 BC)**, and a **clay pyxis linked to
    funerary ritual (1300–1250 BC)**. [medium] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_Chania

## 5. Maritime Museum of Crete and the ship *Minoa*

- **Maritime Museum of Crete:** its main building is at the **Firkas fortress, western end of the harbour**. It
  holds a Bronze Age room with models of Minoan ships and finds from underwater archaeology. [medium]
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Museum_of_Crete
- ***Minoa*:** an **experimental replica of a 16th–15th c. BC Minoan ship**, about **17 m long and about 4 m
  wide**. It was built **2001–2004**: **split cypress planks lashed and sewn together, not nailed**, with **oars
  and a single square sail**. It was **launched December 2003** and in **2004 rowed and sailed about 210
  nautical miles from Chania to Piraeus** for the **Athens Olympics**, linking up with the Olympic Flame
  (carried for the last leg by the trireme *Olympias*). [high for the voyage; medium for the exact dimensions
  and launch month] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Museum_of_Crete ·
  https://greece.terrabook.com/chania/page/minoan-ship/ ·
  https://www.cretanbeaches.com/en/history-of-crete/museums/historical-museums-and-collections/museum-of-ancient-and-traditional-shipbuilding
- **Where it is now:** in the **Neorio Moro** (a 16th-c. Venetian shipyard arsenal) at the **eastern end of the
  harbour**. It is shown with the Exhibition of Ancient and Traditional Shipbuilding. The design drew on ship
  images such as the **Akrotiri (Thera) "Flotilla" fresco** and seals. [high for location; medium for the design
  sources]
- **Tour line:** stand at the Firkas end with the Master Impression's "sea town" in mind, then walk to the
  Neorio and look at a ship of the sort the man on the seal would have watched come in.

## 6. Daily life: how a Minoan town worked

- **Food:** barley and emmer/einkorn wheat, **legumes** (the burnt lentils and vetch at Kydonia),
  **olive oil**, **wine** (grape pips, wine jars), **honey** (the Zeus/Dionysos offering was *honey*),
  figs, sheep and goat for milk, cheese and wool, pigs, fish and shellfish, and wild greens (Cretans still forage
  *horta*). Cooking happened on hearths and in tripod cooking pots. [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization
- **Saffron:** crocus gathering is shown on the **"Saffron Gatherers" fresco at Akrotiri (Thera)**, where girls
  pick crocus for a seated goddess. It was used for dye, medicine and ritual. Its exact economic scale is
  debated. [high for the fresco] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_civilization
- **Storage = wealth:** huge **pithoi** (storage jars) held oil, grain and wine. Sealings on jar stoppers,
  boxes and doors controlled who could open what. The Kydonia sealings show a **bureaucracy of locks**. [high]
- **Houses:** multi-storey, of rubble and timber framing with plastered walls. Ground floors were storerooms and
  workrooms; living rooms were upstairs (the Linear A tablets fell *from the upper floor* of Building 1). There
  were light wells, flat roofs, and paved streets with drains. The Master Impression's tall windowed buildings
  match this. [high]
- **Textiles:** **loom weights** are found in houses (the Kydonia kitchen loom). Wool was the backbone of the
  Linear B economy, and **named women and children appear as textile workers** in Knossos records (ration lists).
  Minoan purple dye was made from murex shells. [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B
- **Women's roles:** prominent in ritual imagery (priestesses, processions, the "Ladies in Blue"), in textile
  production and in Linear B ration lists. Their *political* power is **unknown and CONTESTED**.
- **Religion as practised:** small house shrines with benches, clay figurines left at peak sanctuaries and caves,
  libation tables (often inscribed in Linear A), offerings of oil and honey, the horns of consecration on
  rooftops. Ordinary people took part. It was not just palace ritual. [high] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_religion
- **Writing:** Linear A (and the earlier Cretan hieroglyphic) is **undeciphered**. The numbers and some
  commodity signs can be read, so we can read the *accounts* but not the *words*. [high]

## Story hooks

### For Rob (story, D&D flavour, monsters, labyrinths, ships, bronze giants)
1. **Talos, Crete's coast guard.** A bronze golem that runs the island's perimeter three times a day, stoning
   any ship that approaches. One weak point: a vein at the ankle sealed with a bronze nail. Medea kills him with
   the evil eye. He is the boss fight of the *Argonautica*, written by Apollonius *of Rhodes* in the Colossus's
   own century. Rhodes had its bronze giant as a monument; Crete's walked.
2. **The Master Impression = a player character's signet.** A giant figure looms over a walled sea-town.
   The gold ring that stamped it has *never been found*. Somewhere under Chania or in a looter's cache is the
   town's master seal. Instant quest hook.
3. **The burned library.** The fire of c. 1450 BC baked ~99 Linear A tablets on Kastelli hard. They are a
   ledger in a language no one can read. We can count the sheep but not say their names.
4. **Gods on the receipt.** A 13th-c. BC Chania tablet: honey to Zeus, *double* honey to Dionysos.
   Ask "who was the bigger deal in Kydonia?" Party-god outbids sky-god.
5. **Diktynna's leap.** Pursued by Minos for nine months, she jumps off a cliff and is saved in fishermen's
   nets. Her temple is on the Rodopou peninsula, visible from Chania's waterfront: the "net goddess" watching
   over the gulf.
6. **Grandson of Minos.** Kydon, Chania's legendary founder, is the son of Minos's daughter. If his mother
   Akakallis was Pasiphae's daughter, the Minotaur (Pasiphae's son by the bull) was Kydon's half-uncle. That
   follows from the genealogy; no ancient text says it [low]. Family tree as dungeon map.
7. **Ship *Minoa*.** Built like the ships Minos's "first navy" would have used: lashed cypress, no nails. Real
   rowers took it 210 nautical miles to Piraeus for the 2004 Olympics.

### For Jamie (households, work, food, religion as lived practice, women, writing and bureaucracy)
1. **A kitchen 3,500 years old.** In Kanevaro St's Building 1: a clay hearth with a loom right beside it, and a
   pot of lentils and vetch burnt in the fire. Cooking and weaving in one room, the same rhythm as a Cretan
   village kitchen.
2. **Bureaucracy in clay.** Sealings on jars and doors, a clay bar stamped twelve times by ten different seals.
   Someone checked who opened the storeroom. Then Linear B: women textile workers listed by name for rations.
3. **The quince.** The town gave the fruit its name, "Kydonian apple", and through it English "quince" and
   marmalade (from Portuguese *marmelo*, quince). A Cretan sweet-spoon quince (*kydoni glyko*) makes a good
   tasting stop.
4. **Honey for the gods.** Offerings of honey written up on the same kind of tablets as sheep and wool. Religion
   was part of the household economy, budgeted like everything else.
5. **Upstairs, downstairs.** Storerooms and workshops on the ground floor, living and record-keeping upstairs.
   The tablets fell through the burning floor. That tells you where the "office" was.
6. **Women at work, not on thrones.** Strong evidence for women's ritual and economic roles; the "matriarchy" story
   is a 20th-c. projection. A good honest-history moment.

## Corrections to the brief, and things to flag
- ***ku-do-ni-ja*** is secure on **Knossos** tablets. I could not confirm it on a tablet found *at* Chania.
- The **Armeni** larnakes are from a cemetery near **Rethymno**, not Chania. They are shown in Chania's museum
  (and in Rethymno's).
- The **Diktynnaion** remains are **Archaic to Roman (7th c. BC–Hadrian)**. Calling it a Minoan sanctuary is not
  supported. A goddess with Minoan roots is plausible.
- The **old** Chania Archaeological Museum (San Francesco, Chalidon St) no longer holds the collection. Since
