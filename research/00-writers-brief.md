# Writers' brief: Chania Landfall, two reading tracks

Assembled from 01-logistics.md, 02-history.md and 03-minoan.md. Read all three before writing; use only facts they support, prefer [high] facts, and never present a [low] or contested item as settled.

## The day and the readers

- Rob and Jamie are on Virgin Voyages' Scarlet Lady. The wind diverted the ship from Mykonos to Souda, so Chania is an unplanned bonus day: Friday 2 October 2026, ashore 08:00 to 18:00. Two days ago they toured Rhodes with a sibling guide (Rob's birthday was that day), where the Colossus, the Knights and the sieges were the story. Light callbacks to Rhodes are welcome (the Colossus as Rhodes's bronze giant, Talos as Crete's; Apollonius *of Rhodes* wrote the Talos episode). Don't overdo it.
- Weather today is unusually bad: about 18 °C, north wind gusting near 30 km/h, showers from mid-morning, thunderstorms from about 13:00. The route puts outdoors first and museums after lunch. One or two nods to the weather are fine (a harbour that has seen worse); don't make it a theme.
- These tracks are for READING on a phone, not for speech. Digits, dates as numbers, and the odd parenthesis are fine. No headings, no bullet lists, no markdown: plain paragraphs separated by a blank line (in JSON, "\n\n").
- Rob's track: story, D&D flavour, factions, sieges, ships, monsters and bronze giants, the "quest hook" in a place. Vivid but accurate. Second person, present tense where it helps ("Stand with your back to...").
- Jamie's track: how ordinary people organised their lives in each era. Households, work, food, money and records, religion as lived practice, neighbours of different faiths living side by side, women's work, what changed for whom when the flags changed. Warm, plain, curious; still second person and anchored in what's in front of you.
- "Minoan mojo" runs through both tracks, not just the Minoan stops: Kydonia under the hill, the quince, the Linear B honey for Zeus and Dionysos, the Master Impression, Talos, Diktynna's nets, the ship Minoa, ships and the sea as the island's long constant. Flag what's myth as myth and what's contested as contested (no "Minoan matriarchy" or "Minoan peace" as fact; the Minotaur and Labyrinth belong to Knossos, not Chania; the name ku-do-ni-ja is securely attested on Knossos tablets, not proven on a Chania tablet).
- Each stop's text should open by orienting the reader to what's physically in front of them, and the long version should end by pointing them to the next stop (direction and roughly how far).

## Lengths

- `short`: 90 to 140 words. A minute at the spot: what you're looking at, the one story that matters, where next.
- `long`: 500 to 750 words. The full story for the spot, three to five minutes' reading.

## The ten stops (fixed order, fixed slugs)

1. `souda` — **Souda Bay.** Read on the shuttle or bus (25 minutes into town). The great natural harbour; Akrotiri peninsula to the north; the Venetian fortress islet that stayed Venetian until 1715, seventy years after Chania fell; the Battle of Crete in May 1941 and the Commonwealth war cemetery on the shore; a NATO base today (warships across the water). Today is a diversion: ships have always come here when the weather said so. Minoan: the sea routes, Talos who circled the island three times a day stoning strange ships (a good opening for Rob as they arrive).
2. `agora` — **The Market Hall (Agora).** Where the shuttle or bus 13 likely sets down. Cross-shaped hall of 1911 to 1913, built on a demolished Venetian bastion; opened by Venizelos days after the 1 December 1913 union with Greece. CLOSED for restoration today (reopening announced for December 2026): see it from outside. A good place to set up Chania's layers: Minoan, Greek and Roman Kydonia, Byzantine, Venetian 1252 to 1645, Ottoman 1645 to 1898 (with the Egyptian interlude 1830s to 1840), the Cretan State 1898 to 1913 with Chania as capital, then Greece. Walk north-east about 7 minutes to Splantzia.
3. `splantzia` — **Splantzia: Plateia 1821 and Agios Nikolaos.** Dominican church (c. 1320), the sultan's mosque after 1645, Orthodox again in 1918; a minaret and a bell tower on one building. The plane tree (the hanging-tree story is LEGEND; say so). The Muslim quarter; the 1923 population exchange emptied it. Coffee or raki at Platanos. Then about 4 minutes west, uphill, to Kastelli.
4. `kastelli` — **Kastelli hill: Minoan Kydonia.** The excavations seen through fences on Kanevaro Street and Agia Aikaterini square. The heart of the Minoan material: the destruction fire around 1450 BC that baked the Linear A archive; the hearth and loom and burnt lentils; the Master Impression (giant figure over a walled sea-town; the ring that made it has never been found); the Linear B tablet KH Gq 5 with honey for Zeus and Dionysos; Kydon the founder; Homer's Kydonians; the quince named for the town. Byzantine and Venetian walls on top. Then down about 4 minutes to the Neoria on the east harbour.
5. `neoria` — **The Venetian shipyards (Neoria) and the ship Minoa.** Seventeen vaults on the south side plus the Moro yards to the east (sources give 22 or 23 in all: don't pick one as certain); galleys hauled in for building and winter repair; in 1645 the Ottomans found galleys inside. The Grand Arsenal just west. The Minoa in the Moro yard (Maritime Museum annex, €3): a nail-free lashed replica of a Bronze Age ship, rowed and sailed from Chania to Piraeus for the 2004 Olympics. Then out along the breakwater, about 10 minutes, to the lighthouse.
6. `lighthouse` — **The lighthouse.** Venetian light at the harbour mouth from the 16th century; rebuilt in the 19th century (local accounts say under the Egyptian administration around 1839; other sources say 1864: say sources differ), which is why it looks a bit like a minaret; damaged in 1941; restored in the 2000s. The view back over the whole waterfront. Safety: skip it if the breakwater is wet and spray is coming over. Diktynna, the goddess of nets, whose later sanctuary is on the Rodopou peninsula to the west (not a proven Minoan site). Then back along the breakwater and round the harbour, about 15 minutes, to the domed mosque.
7. `mosque` — **Kioutsouk Hasan mosque (Yiali Tzamisi).** Built just after the 1645 conquest, the oldest Ottoman building on Crete; named for the first Ottoman commander; lost its minaret in the 20th century; opens only for exhibitions now. The 1645 siege (about two months: give the range the research gives), the 1644 pirate raid by the Knights of Malta that gave the Sultan his pretext, the 21-year siege of Candia that followed, Blasio Zulian blowing up the islet fort rather than surrender. Then west along the harbour, about 5 minutes, to Firkas.
8. `firkas` — **Firkas fortress and the Maritime Museum of Crete.** Venetian bastion at the west mouth of the harbour; Ottoman barracks and prison; the Greek flag raised here on 1 December 1913 for union with Greece. The museum (09:00 to 17:00, €4): Bronze Age ship models, Venetian galleys, the Battle of Crete gallery. The revolts of 1866 to 1897, the Cretan State, Venizelos the rebel turned prime minister. Then about 5 minutes south into Topanas and the old Jewish quarter.
9. `synagogue` — **Etz Hayyim synagogue.** Open today until 15:00, €4 with a short tour by the staff. A Venetian church that became the synagogue of Evraiki, the Jewish quarter; Romaniote community with roots on Crete going back to antiquity; the community's arrest in May 1944 and the sinking of the Tanais on 9 June 1944 by a British submarine that didn't know who was aboard (give the figures carefully: sources range widely); the building's restoration and reopening in 1999. Write this one with care and without D&D flavour on Rob's track. Then lunch nearby (Tamam is two minutes away), and a taxi east to the museum.
10. `museum` — **The Archaeological Museum of Chania (Chalepa).** The rainy-afternoon stop, open 08:00 to 20:00, €15 with audio guide; opened in 2022 at Skra 15. What to look for: the Linear A and Linear B tablets from Kastelli, the Master Impression, Minoan pottery and coffins (an Armeni coffin is on show; Armeni is near Rethymno, not Chania), and the later Greek and Roman city. Close the day: the Minoan thread that started under the morning's hill; Chalepa as the consuls' quarter of the Cretan State. End with the way home: a taxi from here to Souda is the easiest way back to the ship before 18:00.
